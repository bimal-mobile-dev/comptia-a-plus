- **21% weighting.** The professionalism, documentation, and safety domain — the least "technical" domain on either exam, but still heavily scenario-tested

## Documentation and change management

- **Ticketing and documentation**: properly documenting a support incident, including the problem description, resolution steps, and any follow-up needed
- **Change management basics**: understanding why IT changes should go through a documented, approved process even in a small support environment — a lighter version of the change management concepts covered in depth across the security certifications in this collection

## Safety and environmental procedures

- **Electrical and physical safety**: proper handling of hardware to prevent injury and equipment damage, including electrostatic discharge (ESD) prevention when handling internal components
- **Environmental controls**: proper disposal of batteries, toner, and other hazardous materials in compliance with relevant regulations

## Professionalism and communication

- **Professional communication techniques**: appropriate communication with end users, including active listening, avoiding jargon with non-technical users, and maintaining a professional demeanor even in frustrating support situations
- **Prohibited content and remote access best practices**: recognizing and properly handling prohibited/illegal content encountered during support work, and following secure procedures when using remote access tools to support a user's device

## Basic scripting awareness

- **Scripting basics for automation**: recognizing common scripting file types and their basic purpose (automating repetitive support tasks) — introductory awareness rather than the deeper scripting/programming skill expected at higher-level certifications
